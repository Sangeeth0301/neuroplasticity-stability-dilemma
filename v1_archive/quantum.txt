The optimal project: Plasticity-Aware Quantum Continual Learning
This is real and it's hot. Quantum Continual Learning (QCL) is an established subfield — quantum machine learning models built on variational quantum circuits (VQCs) have been empirically shown to suffer catastrophic forgetting when learning tasks in sequence, just like classical networks. It's been demonstrated on real hardware: a superconducting-processor experiment sequentially trained a quantum classifier on three tasks and used elastic weight consolidation (EWC) to retain knowledge across all three with over 92% average accuracy.
Here's the gap, and it's almost embarrassingly perfect for you: a paper published essentially this week (PRX Quantum, "Intrinsic Preservation of Plasticity in Continual Quantum Learning") states plainly that while the stability side of quantum continual learning (not forgetting) has been studied extensively, the plasticity side — a quantum model's ability to keep learning new tasks well over time — remains unexplored. That's the stability-plasticity dilemma, the exact same term neuroscientists use for biological neuroplasticity, sitting unresolved in a paper from days ago.
Your project: design a plasticity-preserving regularization/architecture for VQCs — you could adapt ideas from the biologically-grounded astrocyte-gated multi-timescale plasticity mechanism we discussed, translating it into a quantum-circuit analog — then benchmark on real IBM hardware against EWC (the current default) and against classical continual-learning baselines. You'd be directly extending a nearly-brand-new open problem, in a subfield that already has hardware precedent, using your neuroplasticity research angle as the actual novel mechanism. This is the strongest, most fundable, most "why you and not someone else" project in this whole conversation.
Feasibility: small VQCs (8-16 qubits) for classification tasks run in seconds; comfortably inside IBM's free 10-minutes/month Open Plan.
Target venue: PRX Quantum, npj Quantum Information, or Quantum Machine Intelligence — all have published near-identical-scope work in the last year.

The deepest project: ML-optimized search for the Pólya-Hilbert operator
This is the one we scoped earlier — genuinely the hardest and most foundational thing in this whole thread. Using a VQE-style circuit, optimized via classical ML rather than hand-derived physics, to search for an operator whose eigenvalue spectrum reproduces Riemann zeta zero statistics, benchmarked against the 2021 trapped-ion Floquet realization and 2025/2026 SUSY/acceleration-Hamiltonian proposals. It won't solve the Riemann Hypothesis — nothing at this level will — but it's a real, citable, mathematically deep contribution, and it's the project that would impress a very different kind of reviewer (pure theory/math-physics) than the QCL one.
